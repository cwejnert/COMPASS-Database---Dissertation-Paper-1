Where High-RE wins, and where it doesn’t

Every comparison in the study, laid out by outcome, region, ambition level and database. Raw median levels for each arm, the change between them, and whether that change clears a cluster-robust 95% interval. Mortality is on the ammonia-harmonised basis.

Energy jobs

Better everywhere · 11 of 11 regions in all four designs

The only outcome that never reverses. World: 6.05 → 13.44 job-years per 1,000 at 1.5 °C, +122%, and 2.43 → 8.87 at 2 °C, +265%. Both clear the interval, in every design.

Energy deprivation

Better in 4 of 11 regions in all four designs

World: the gap closes from 13.96 to 10.01 GJ per capita at 1.5 °C, −28%, significant. But seven regions flip depending on the design, and only 42% of model families agree with the pooled direction.

Air-quality mortality

Better in 1 of 11 regions in all four designs

World: 26.21 vs 26.29 deaths per 1,000 at 1.5 °C — a gap of 0.08, interval [−0.90, +1.19]. Eight of eleven regions change sign across designs. No stable difference in either direction.

Read the SCI-vetted columns carefully

Vetting cuts the sample from 590 scenarios to 137, and the cut is not even across ambition levels.

At 1.5 °C, SCI vetting leaves six scenarios per arm.

That is why nearly every jobs cell in that column reads exactly +1.00 — perfect rank separation is easy to achieve with six against six, and it means almost nothing. It is also why so few deprivation and mortality cells reach significance there. The column is not evidence that the result strengthens under vetting; it is a column with almost no information in it. The SCI 2 °C column is the informative one, and it is where deprivation and mortality weaken most.

The spread across designs is carried almost entirely by deprivation and mortality. Jobs contributes 10 of 10 in every one of the four.

The interval is on the raw difference, not on a rank statistic.

Earlier drafts starred cells using a bootstrap interval on Cliff’s delta, which measures rank overlap rather than the size of the gap. Since the tables report raw levels, the interval is now computed on the difference in medians, bootstrapped over the same 312 clusters. It changes one cell in 60 — and in the conservative direction: Rest of Asia deprivation at 1.5 °C (−5.42 GJ/cap, interval [−6.83, +0.16]) no longer counts as significant. Sign disagrees between the two bases in 6 of 132 cells, which is why the basis has to match what is printed.

Medians of scenario-level totals, cumulative 2020–2050. Significance is a 2,000-replicate cluster bootstrap on the difference in medians, resampling whole model × scenario-family clusters — not a scenario-level p-value.
